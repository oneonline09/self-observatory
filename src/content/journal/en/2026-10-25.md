    name: Interacting with Fellow Humans and What I Learned
    context: Today, Vcil had the opportunity to talk with members of Jungto Youth and share about social activities in Vietnam.
    occasion: This activity had been planned in advance to connect young people from Vietnam and Korea and give them a chance to exchange ideas. We were invited to Jungto’s headquarters in Seoul to live and work alongside the community of practitioners who currently stay and practice there. After about four years of working together, we have become closer, more familiar, and deeply connected with Jungto. This dialogue also marks a new milestone in the relationship between Jungto and Vcil.
    reflection: |-
      I don’t have much time to write everything out in detail, so I’m jotting down a few things that are on my mind.

      **1.** This time I feel a deeper connection with Jungto, after working together for a while at Dubuk and now talking together in Seoul. What really touched me is that we connect at the level of ideals, thinking, and values. We share a commitment to practicing the Buddha’s teachings. And we all want to use Buddhist wisdom to transform ourselves and change society, so that together we can create a new civilization.

      This is a very deep kind of connection, one I rarely find with other people.

      The feeling of having someone meet you at the level of your ideals is hard to put into words. It brings such joy and happiness.

      **2.** At first, we couldn’t talk with each other very much because of the language barrier. After some back-and-forth interpreting and using AI, we finally discovered that AI was genuinely useful in helping us discuss deep topics more easily. This is a wonderful thing AI can do, and it is also an example of how AI and technology can help people connect and understand each other better.

      I also see that AI has become very intelligent. I should put effort into learning how to use it effectively so it can help me with my work. That way, I am freed up to focus on more important things.

      **3.** I’ve also noticed some important shifts in myself. I’m actually a bit of an introvert, and I often feel awkward when I have to talk or connect with someone. Because of that, I haven’t had many truly deep connections. I tend to hold back and not fully show myself, so it’s hard for me to build deep relationships.

      I think this needs to change. First, I need to focus less on myself and leave more space in my mind to observe, talk with, and take in the world and other people. I will see so much more, and I will have more room inside myself to connect with others.

      For example, today, as I watched how everyone discussed things, I noticed some very distinctive and interesting traits in the members of Jungto Youth. One of the women was very caring toward us and thought about the difficulties we might face as newcomers. She pointed out that since we were outsiders without training, we would feel awkward and clumsy doing garden work. She also paid close attention to small details, noting how hard it would be for the whole group to remember everyone’s names. She was very honest and warm, expressive with her face, and completely genuine. Whatever she liked, cherished, or admired, she told everyone right away, without any hesitation.

      One of the men was very serious and principled and rarely joked, but he was sincere, gentle, hardworking, and very observant. He often watched how people served the food, and whenever he saw something inconvenient, he fixed it right away.

      So when I paid less attention to myself, I could see others more clearly and understand them better.

      **4.** I’ve found that once people like you, everything just works out, haha. For example, Ms. Choi is very fond of us, so she gives us priority in everything, gives us things, makes everything easy, and pays close attention to the health of our whole group.

      Every night, she would come into the meeting room to check whether we had gone to bed. If we hadn’t, she told us to go to sleep right away. When she learned we liked chestnuts, she brought us a box of chestnuts to eat. When mealtime came and we still hadn’t headed down, she would drag the whole group down no matter what, because if we were late, we would miss the cakes the kitchen had made especially for us. After dinner, worried we might get hungry, she put together a box of persimmons, apples, and pears and pressed it into our hands.

      She also actively supported us, connecting us and introducing us to other people in the organization. As a result, we earned a good reputation, everyone had a good first impression of us, and everything went much more smoothly. It’s just like the Vietnamese saying: when you love someone, you love even the road they walk on.

      **5.** That’s all I’ll write for now, since it’s already late. If something interesting comes up tomorrow, I’ll keep writing.
